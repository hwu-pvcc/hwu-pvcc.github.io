A few “Big Tech” dominate the industry, leading to severe problems: Major tech companies, like Amazon, Apple, Google, Meta (Facebook), and Microsoft, hold vast control over different sectors of technology, from search engines and social media to e-commerce and cloud services. This concentration can stifle competition, making it difficult for startups or smaller companies to thrive. With limited competition, innovation may slow down, and consumers may have fewer choices. Customers are severely exploited. However, as individual, it’s practically impossible to safeguard their interest versus big tech.

Do you know tech monopoly is everywhere?

Amazon : Amazon will account for 40.4% of US retail ecommerce sales or a total of $491.65 billion in 2024 and controls nearly 70% of the ebook market. In 2023, the Federal Trade Commission and 17 states attorneys general sued Amazon.com, Inc. They sued amazon because amazon violated the antitrust law by engaging in a course of exclusionary conduct that prevents current competitors from growing and new competitors from emerging. By stifling competition on price, product selection, quality, and by preventing its current or future rivals from attracting a critical mass of shoppers and sellers. Amazon ensures no current or future rival can threaten its dominance, namely monopoly. Amazon’s far-touch schemes impact hundreds of billions of dollars in retail sales every year, touch hundreds of thousands of products sold by businesses big and small and affect over a hundred million shoppers.

Apple : Apple had been sued for several monopolies conduct including:

  • Diminishing the functionality of non-apple smartwatches, which essentially bundles the purchase of apple phone and smartwatch, and make it difficult to for third party smartwatches companies to sell their products to iPhone users.
  • Excluding cross-platform messaging apps by making these apps less innovative, less secure and less functional so that its customers have to keep buying iPhones.
  • Limiting third-party digital wallets. Apple has prevented third-party apps from offering tap-to-pay functionality; Apple forbade the usage of google wallet in apple products. These conducts make the apple wallet more superior than any other digital wallet.

Why is so hard to solve the tech monopoly problem?
  • Small companies lack popularity and resources

    Big tech owns two properties that small companies are hard to possess: popularity and resources.

    First, new tech is hard to earn popularity among users due to fierce competition. These tech magnates preempted the market and became magnates earlier when there was barely any competition because they were the first to come up with innovative ideas. Now, many companies operate in similar business areas, creating fierce competition, making it nearly impossible for new companies to gain enough popularity to compete.

    Second, start-up tech companies are not competitive compared to large tech companies due to the lack of resources. Users prefer services like YouTube, where the most comprehensive resources are available, and creators flock there because it has the largest audience. This cycle further enlarges big companies and stifles the growth of smaller ones.

  • Monopoly is hard to define in digital scope

    It's not easy to define and implement every aspect of antitrust law in digital scope. There are many gray areas in digital which are hard to define as monopoly. Like what I state before, the popularity and resources are essentially monopoly, but it’s impossible to define them. Bundle purchase, such as Microsoft’s built-in utility software purchase, is defined and sued. However, there are many bundle purchases, such as iPhone’s built-in utility software purchase, are not even considered as monopoly.

  • The current antitrust law can be easily bypassed in digital world by alternatives.

    It's not hard for big tech to use alternatives to avoid and bypass them to achieve the same monopoly purpose. Form example, the DMA requires Apple to allow alternative app stores on the iPhone or alternative distribution channels for installing an app. To comply, Apple has removed its technical prohibition against the installation of third-party apps/app stores and instead instituted a core technology fee under which developers of third-part app stores and third-party apps pay 0.50 fee per installed app. This hinders the growth of the third-party app stores or apps.

Have we solved the problem?

Unfortunately, we haven’t come up with effective solutions to conquer this crux. That’s why tech monopoly is still so pervasive today.

What can we do?

We can make more people be aware of this problem and its danger. By doing this, we hope to make executives in big tech to have more moral integrity to stop monopoly conducts, and draw more public attention so the public can influence legislature to make more comprehensive and feasible law.
